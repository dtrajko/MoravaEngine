MoravaEngine · a game on the engine

Racing

The plan for a fast, fun arcade racing game on Jolt's vehicle physics, inspired by Trackmania's style but simpler: one closed circuit built in 3D (climbs, drops, banked turns), three laps against the clock and your own best lap, instant restart, respawn at the last checkpoint and medals. It is meant to be fun and not too hard, not a simulation.

Agreed November 8, 2026, after the vehicle work (the engine guide). It replaces the Driving School plan as the next thing to build; that plan stays here, unbuilt. Names that exist in the code are written as they are. Names of new classes, files and settings are proposals. This page is kept up to date with "As built", "Checked" and "Not done" boxes, as the engine guide is.

The decisions

The user's

  • Fast, fun, not too hard: arcade handling, not a simulation.
  • The track is modeled in Blender, by a script. Tools/TrackBuilder/build_track.py runs in Blender without its window (blender --background --python). It reads the track's data, a closed list of control points, builds the meshes and exports a .glb the engine imports. The data file is the source: a change made by hand in Blender is lost the next time the script runs.
  • The first track's elements: slopes and banked turns. Jumps, boost pads and a loop come later.
  • Laps on a circuit: three laps, lap times, the best lap and the best race.
  • The car is a free CC0 race car model (first choice: Kenney's Car Kit, glTF, with separate wheels), with its license note in the project.
  • The project is Projects/Racing, next to Sample.
  • Top speed about 350 km/h.
  • Curbs on the inner side of a turn only, not on both sides (2026-11-08).
  • The terrain is only a backdrop: as few triangles as possible; it just has to hide the sky map's lower half (2026-11-08).
  • Trees and foliage outside the track, Trackmania style: real trunks and branches near the camera with leaves on textured cards, 2D billboards at a distance (impostors: Phase 8). The trees are CC0 models (for example from Poly Haven), simplified in Blender to game size.

Proposed and accepted

  • Arcade handling in two layers. The engine adds what only C++ can reach in Jolt: tire grip settings (the friction callback is a fixed 10× now), grip per axle that a script can change while driving (for drifting), gear ratios and top speed. A C# CarController adds the assists: downforce that grows with speed (it holds the car on banked turns and over crests), less steering at speed with quick self-centering, a drift on the brake and handbrake (the rear grip drops), air control (tilting in the air, gentle leveling) and slippery walls (the car scrapes along them instead of stopping). Targets: 0–100 km/h in about 2 s, top speed about 350 km/h, full throttle through the banked turns.
  • The track's data drives everything. Each control point has a position, a road width, a bank angle and flags (walls, curbs, supports). The script also writes the checkpoint gates and the start grid, and the center line (a point every few meters) into the project. The checkpoints are the respawn points.
  • A scripted test driver follows the center line through Vehicle.SetInput (Controls: Script). It checks every change to the track and the handling unattended, and gives the first medal times. Later it is the basis of AI opponents.
  • The setting: a raised circuit on supports above wide ground, with the seeded terrain (vehicle part A) as hills around it.
  • The ghost of the best lap comes after the core game works (Phase 9).

The performance target

On the user's machine: an RTX 3070 Ti, a Ryzen 9 5900X and a 2560×1440 display at 180 Hz.

  • Medium (the game's default tier, no ray tracing) aims at 120 fps (8.3 ms a frame) and is never under 60 (16.7 ms), counted on the 1% lows, not only the average.
  • High (ray tracing) and Ultra: never under 60.

How the plan keeps it:

  • A budget check in every phase. The test driver laps the track at Medium and High and logs the frame times (average, 1% low, the worst frame), with the GPU profiler's time for each pass. A phase isn't done while a tier is below its target, so a cost is seen in the phase that adds it.
  • Phase 1 measures the baseline (the test track, the car, the terrain) before anything else is added.
  • Known costs, fixed early: the terrain had no level of detail and took about 6.6 ms of the frame; it got levels of detail in Phase 1 (done 2026-11-08). The track is exported in chunks of about 100 m, so it is culled chunk by chunk in the view and in each shadow cascade.
  • The foliage is built for the budget (Phase 8): instancing, billboards (impostors) at a distance, culling per instance, and a density and draw distance per tier.
  • Frame pacing: an FPS cap in Settings (60, 120, 144, 180, none) with VSync on or off, if Settings lacks it. Physics stays at a fixed 60 Hz, and the drawing is interpolated between the steps (the game loop does this already).
  • Render scale (drawing smaller and upscaling with TAA, planned in the quality tiers) only if Medium can't reach the target without it.

Phase 1: the project and the track builder game tools

  • Projects/Racing: Racing.mproject, Settings/Input.minput (the vehicle actions and Respawn, Restart, Pause), Scripts/Racing.csproj built by CMake as Sample's scripts are.
  • Tools/TrackBuilder/build_track.py and a README, with the data in Projects/Racing/Tracks/:
    • a closed centripetal Catmull-Rom spline through the points, sampled about every meter
    • a frame at each sample: the tangent, the right from the world's up, turned by the bank
    • meshes: the road (UVs along its length), curbs, side walls, supports down to the ground, the start and finish line, in chunks of about 100 m
    • named mesh parts (Road, Curb, Wall, Support), which get the project's materials
  • A test track first: an oval with one climb, one drop and one banked turn.
  • The scene: the track model with mesh colliders (the road grips, the walls are slippery), the sun, the environment, the terrain and Sample's car as a stand-in, with Sample's chase camera.
  • Checked: the stand-in car drives the whole lap; the collider matches the drawn road (the physics debug view).
  • The performance baseline at Medium and High; the terrain's level of detail here if the budget needs it.
As built (2026-11-08)
  • Projects/Racing: Racing.mproject (windowed 1600×900 while it is developed, DefaultQuality: Medium, start scene Scenes/TestTrack.mscene), Settings/Input.minput (Accelerate, Brake, Steer, Handbrake, Respawn, Restart, Pause, the camera's Look, Orbit, Zoom, the UI's actions), Scripts/Racing.csproj with VehicleCamera.cs and GameOptions.cs from Sample, the terrain's materials. CMake builds every project's scripts in a loop now (MoravaSampleScripts, MoravaRacingScripts).
  • Tools/TrackBuilder/build_track.py and its README.md: three models, so the colliders can differ: Track_<Name>_Road.glb (road, curbs, start line: friction 1), _Walls.glb (friction 0.05: the car slides along) and _Structure.glb (the slab's sides and underside, the supports: no collider); and <Name>.centerline.json (every 2 m: position, right, up, width; the start). Chunks of 100 m. Curbs on the inner side of a turn only (its direction measured over 20 m before and after). The models' own materials (base colors) are used for now.
  • The test track (Tracks/TestTrack.track.json): 1.2 km, 13 chunks, an 11.6 m climb to a crest, a banked left turn (28°), a drop, a flat left turn; 21 k triangles in all. The scene places it on the terrain's flat area (45.78 m, measured with a ray in Play); the terrain's flat area covers the whole track (Flat Center −80, −25; Flat Radius 290).
  • Checked: a scripted driver along the center line (in a scratch copy, the start of Phase 3's test driver) laps the stand-in car again and again: 39–42 s a lap, at most 2.9 m from the center line, never airborne, no flips. No validation messages.
  • The baseline (Release player, a 2560×1410 window, the driver lapping, --frame-stats, new in the engine for this): Medium 101 fps, 1% low 69; High 92, 1% low 65. The terrain took about 6.6 ms (without it: 305 fps).
  • So the terrain got levels of detail (the engine guide's terrain section, "As built 2026-11-08"): each chunk drawn with as few of its heights as keep it within LOD Tolerance at its distance, from every height to two triangles a chunk, with skirts for the gaps; debug toggles Terrain LOD and Show Terrain LOD in Renderer Stats. The scene uses LOD Tolerance 2. After: Medium 205 fps, 1% low 148; High 172, 1% low 139; no frame under 120 fps in either (Medium had one 47 ms hitch in 42 s, to watch). The frame: 31.5 k triangles, GPU 1.9 ms; the rest of the 4.9 ms is the CPU.
  • Not done: the project's own materials for the track (asphalt and the rest: Phase 7's scenery); the CPU's share of the frame (about 3 ms) is not looked into yet.

As built (2026-11-08)
  • The car: the body of race.glb of Kenney's Car Kit 3.1 (CC0, an open-wheel race car) and four wheel-racing wheels of the same kit (the user's pick: wider, orange rims), in Projects/Racing/Assets/Models/KenneyCarKit/ with the kit's texture (Textures/colormap.png, the palette all its models share; the engine reads it beside a model, not embedded) and its License.txt.

A wheel's own scale is safe: the vehicle keeps a wheel's world scale as it places it.
  • Flat treads (Tools/TrackBuilder/flatten_tires.py, plain Python): the kit's tires are full round only across the middle of their width and chamfer down at both sides, so a widened wheel touched the road with its middle only (the user saw it). The tool writes wheel-racing-flat.glb from wheel-racing.glb: the tread flat across 86% of the width, narrow chamfers, the moved faces' normals made again.
  • Chase camera: farther and higher for the larger car (Distance 9, Height 1.6).
  • Checked: the test driver's laps as with the box car (39.15 s, about 110 km/h on average, 160 km/h at most, at most 2.9 m from the center line); the wheels steer and roll without stretching in the banked turn; the treads on the road across their width; the car's shadow; no validation messages.
  • Not done: the built game's license notices are the engine's (cmake/ThirdPartyNotices.cmake): the project's assets go into them in Phase 10. The car's handling is still part B's defaults (Phase 3).

  • Checked: the test driver laps the test track with no wall stops or flips; the user drives it in the editor's Play.
As built (2026-11-08)
  • Engine (the engine guide's vehicle section, "As built 2026-11-08"): VehicleComponent's Front Grip, Rear Grip, Top Speed, Shift Time, Shift Up RPM; C# Vehicle.SetGrip, LateralSlip, LongitudinalSlip; C# Assets.ReadText, for the center line. The track builder now writes the center line into Assets/Tracks/ (a game's pack holds the Assets folder).
  • CarController.cs (on the car; the Vehicle's Controls: Script): keys turn the wheel gradually and let it return faster; less steering the faster it goes (20% at 250 km/h and above); downforce growing with the square of the speed (1.5 g at 300 km/h); the handbrake above 60 km/h drifts (the rear grip at 50% while held, back over 0.6 s, the throttle stays on), below it is a handbrake; ABS (added after the test showed locked wheels in hard braking: the brake eases while a wheel locks); in the air, the throttle and brake pitch it, the steering turns it, and a damped spring rights it. Every assist has its fields in the Inspector.
  • The car's numbers (the scene): 900 Nm, 6000 rpm, Top Speed 360 km/h (final drive 5.09: the real top is about 345, the tires slip a little), shifts in 0.15 s at 5600 rpm, brakes 2500 Nm a wheel, springs 3 Hz with 0.25 m of travel, Front Grip 1.3, Rear Grip 1.4, the center of mass 0.4 m lower, air drag (Linear Damping) 0.015, Angular Damping 0.3.
  • TestDriver.cs: a plain class the car controller drives with when its Test Driver field is on (scripts can't reach each other's instances): it follows the center line (read with Assets.ReadText), slows for the curves ahead (sideways 14 m/s², braking 20 m/s²) and logs its laps, 0–100 and 0–200 km/h.

In the air (dropped from 30 m, rolled 50°) it rights itself and lands level. Release player, the driver lapping: Medium 212 fps, 1% low 165; High 176, 1% low 142; no frame under 120.
  • Not checked by a script: the drift and the keyboard's steering (the test driver neither drifts nor uses keys): the user's test in the editor's Play.

Phase 4: race logic game

  • RaceManager.cs: F1-style start lights (below), checkpoints in order (triggers; a lap with a missed one doesn't count), three laps, lap and race timers, a split at each checkpoint against the best lap (green faster, red slower), respawn at the last checkpoint (on the gate, facing along the track, speed zero), restart, finish.
  • F1-style start lights (the user's choice, 2026-11-08): five red lights at the top of the screen come on one per second, stay on for a random 0.2–1 s, and all go out together: Go. The car is held (brakes on, no throttle) until then; the race timer starts at lights out. The lights are UI images of a generated circle texture on a dark panel; Phase 6 adds a beep at each light and another at lights out; Phase 7 may add the lights to a start gantry over the track. Changed 2026-11-09 (the user's choice, Phase 5): three lights, red, yellow, green, one at a time, a second each after a second's settle; green is go (no random hold), it stays on a second, then the lights hide.
  • Fallen off (the user's request, 2026-11-08: a car that left the track could drive on the terrain below it): an automatic respawn at the last checkpoint, no key needed, when the car is more than a few meters below the road or well outside its edge (from the center line: its height and width there) for about a second, or touches the terrain (the track never runs on it).
  • Wrong way (the user's request, 2026-11-08): a warning while the car drives against the track's direction. From the center line (read with Assets.ReadText): the track's direction at the nearest point against the car's velocity, more than about 100° apart at more than about 15 km/h for more than about 1 s (a spin or a quick turn doesn't set it off); gone as soon as it drives the right way. A large "Wrong way" text in the middle of the screen; Phase 5 styles it with the HUD.
  • Medals: bronze, silver, gold and author times per track, set from test laps.
  • The best lap, the best race and the medals saved with SaveData.
  • Checked: a scripted race of three laps finishes with the right times; respawn and restart from every checkpoint.
As built (2026-11-08)
  • Walls first (the user's report: a wall hit bounced the car off and turned it around): the test track's walls are now 2 m high and 1 m thick (the track data's WallHeight, WallThickness; the 1 m walls were lower than the car's body, whose corner caught on their top and levered it over), and CarController.cs has a wall assist: touching an entity tagged Wall (the track's Walls entity), for 0.3 s its speed into the wall goes, 15% of its speed per second is lost, its turn is held under 60°/s and its roll and pitch from the hit are damped (fields WallTag, WallSpeedKept, WallMaxTurn). Scratch tests: 12° into a wall at 200 km/h, 183 km/h after and flat; 30°, 152 km/h and along the wall in 0.5 s.
  • RaceManager.cs on a Race entity (its UI canvas holds the start lights and the wrong-way text): everything about the track comes from its center line (Centerline.cs, shared with the test driver). Checkpoints are distances along it, not triggers: Sectors (4) parts of equal length, a checkpoint passed when the car's nearest point on the line moves over it, in order (a lap with one missed doesn't count, logged); nothing to place in the scene, and nothing a car at 350 km/h (1.6 m a physics step) can jump through. The car starts behind the line: the timer starts at lights out and the first lap ends at the second time over the line.
  • Start lights: five UI images of a generated circle (Assets/Textures/UI/StartLight.png) on a dark panel at the top: the first after 1 s, one a second, all out after a random 0.2–1 s; the car held (CarController.Held: brakes on, the throttle ignored, the test driver waits) until then; the panel hides 1 s after.
  • Splits: at each checkpoint and the line, the time in the lap against the best lap's there (logged; LastSplit, LastSplitDelta for the HUD).
  • Respawn (Backspace, B): on the last checkpoint passed (the line, or the start before it), 2 m past it, 1 m above the road, facing along it, standing still; the timer runs on. Restart (Enter, Back): the car behind the line, the lights again. The car's script does the move (CarController.Respawn); the race reaches the car's script through CarController.Current (scripts of one game share their static fields).
  • Fallen off: respawned when more than 3 m below the road or 2 m outside its walls for 1 s, or at once on the terrain (tagged Terrain): a short ray down from the car (standing on its wheels its body doesn't touch the ground: Jolt's wheels are casts, not bodies), or its body touching it.
  • Wrong way: the car's velocity (along the road) more than 100° from the track's direction at its nearest point, above 15 km/h, for 1 s: a big red "WRONG WAY" in the middle of the screen; gone as soon as it drives the right way above 15 km/h.
  • Medals (the race's time): author 1:21, gold 1:26, silver 1:38, bronze 2:02, from the test driver's race (1:21.8); Phase 7 sets the real circuit's. Saved (SaveData, by track name): TestTrack.BestLap, BestLapSplits, BestRace, Medal. Pause waits for Phase 5's menu.
  • Checked (scratch copy, the test driver racing, a scratch probe script): a race of three laps (29.37, 26.32, 26.14 s; 1:21.820; the splits against the best lap; the records saved and read at the next start); a respawn 1 s after every checkpoint and the line in two laps, then a restart (the lights again); the car put beside the track (respawned for the fall), far beside it, and set down on the terrain (respawned at once); pushed backwards for 2.5 s (the warning after 1 s, gone when it drove on). Screenshots of the lights and the warning in the player. Release player, the driver racing: Medium 206 fps, 1% low 163; High 172, 1% low 141; no frame under 120. No engine change in this phase.

Phase 6: camera, sound and feel game

  • A chase camera for racing (from VehicleCamera.cs): lower and tighter, a field of view that widens with speed, a small dip on landing, looking ahead in turns.
  • Sounds (CC0): the engine: two loops (low and high revs) crossfaded and pitched by Vehicle.EngineRpm, louder with the throttle; the tires: a squeal from the wheels' slip (LongitudinalSlip braking hard, LateralSlip cornering and drifting); wall scrape (the car's collisions with the walls); the start: a beep as each of the five start lights comes on and another at lights out; checkpoint chime; finish.
  • Built 2026-11-09 (branch racing-feel):
    • Camera: the field of view widens with speed (+12° at 300 km/h), the view looks into turns (the heading's turn rate, a quarter second ahead), dips when the car lands; lower and closer (7.5 m, 1.4 m, 9°); it turns around a point on top of the car (the smoothing had left it meters behind at speed).
    • The engine: no CC0 recording held up stretched over the whole rev range, so it is synthesized (Tools/Audio/GenerateEngineSounds.py): a model measured from a reference recording the user gave (a W16 from 0 to 350 km/h; only measurements were taken): a harmonic series at rpm / 30 with a fixed balance of harmonics and a colored noise that fades out toward idle (the user: a clean idle). Loops every 500 rpm from 1000 to 7000, on and off the throttle; RaceAudio.cs cross-fades the two nearest, each pitched only by rpm / its rpm, the banks by the throttle; an upshift dips the engine and clicks.
    • Gears (the user: first gear too quick): engine setting VehicleComponent.GearRatios (the Inspector's Own Gears; empty: Jolt's five); the car has the Ford GT40's ZF five-speed, 2.23 / 1.61 / 1.24 / 1.00 / 0.81 (the final drive still from Top Speed): first gear 1000 to 5500 rpm in 1.8 s (was 1.0 s), to 91 km/h.
    • Other sounds (Tools/Audio/GenerateRaceSounds.py): start light beeps (red, yellow, green higher), checkpoint chimes (higher when faster than the best lap), the finish, a wall scrape, the tire squeal (narrow wobbling noise bands; chosen by the user from previews). The CC-BY tire recording was not used: the game keeps to CC0.
    • Also: the start line is drawn only (it was in the road's collider, a step the car hopped over); more grip in ordinary driving (Driving Grip 1.5); shifting up at 5500 rpm with the rev counter's red zone at 6000–7000.
  • Manual gears, built 2026-11-09 (the user's request; branch racing-manual-gears): Settings has Gearbox: Automatic / Manual (Automatic the default, kept with the other settings; like the other lists it waits for Apply; also from the pause menu). Manual: the right arrow, = (the + key) or keypad + shifts up, the left arrow, - or keypad − down; the gamepad's bumpers. The arrows then shift instead of steering (A and D steer; with the automatic the arrows still steer: action SteerArrows). The clutch works by itself; from neutral the throttle takes first, and the brake still reverses once stopped. A downshift that would turn the engine past 8000 rpm is refused (the body's speed counts too, so locked wheels in a drift don't let first in at 160 km/h), as is one past the top gear or below first. The HUD shows two shift paddles over the rev counter, − and +, only with the manual gearbox: each press lights its paddle red and pushes it in a little; a refused one lights amber. Engine: Vehicle.ManualGearbox, ShiftUp(), ShiftDown(), GearCount. Checked with the test driver shifting at 6800 rpm: a lap in 28.3 s (the automatic's 29.3 s, which shifts at 5500). Engine braking (the user's request): off the throttle in gear the engine holds the car back, more at high rpm and in a lower gear (engine setting VehicleComponent.EngineBraking, the Inspector's Engine Braking: the share of Engine Torque at Max RPM; the car has 0.2). Lifting off at 140 km/h in 2nd now slows it 3.6 m/s² (1.7 before, mostly the air), at 208 km/h in 4th 2.9 (1.7). Manual only: the automatic is as before. Over-revving, as a GT40 (the user's request): the throttle takes the engine to 7000 rpm, where a rev limiter cuts it; 7000–8000 is for downshifts and engine braking (engine setting VehicleComponent.OverRevRpm, the Inspector's Over-Rev RPM: 1000 on the car; Jolt's limit is Max RPM plus it, the torque curve and the final drive unchanged up to 7000). Downshifts are allowed below about 150 km/h into 1st, 205 into 2nd, 270 into 3rd, 330 into 4th. The rev counter goes to 8, red from 6 (the user's choice). Checked: 1st held on the limiter peaks at 7170 rpm; a downshift from 3rd at 195 km/h goes through and slows the car 5.3 m/s² (lifting alone in 3rd: about 2.8). First gear at the start: the car is put in first when it is placed on the grid and at every respawn (Vehicle.SetGear), checked after restarts from 2nd, 3rd and 4th. Not yet: rebinding the shift keys on the Controls screen (no room left there).

Phase 7: the real circuit game

  • About 2–2.5 km, 35–45 s a lap at up to 350 km/h: a long downhill straight, a climb with a blind crest, two or three steep banked turns, a hairpin, a fast S; 20–30 m between the lowest and the highest point.
  • Walls that hold: a wall height per point in the track builder's data (the 1 m walls can be jumped at speed over a crest or on a banked turn), taller on the outside of fast turns and after crests.
  • Scenery: the terrain's hills around, a start and finish gantry (perhaps with the start lights on it), lighting and reflections tuned.
  • Engine, distance haze (the user's request, 2026-11-09): an Environment option, off by default. Far surfaces fade into the sky's own color near the horizon (sampled from the sky map in the view's direction, so the haze matches any sky), by distance and height (thicker low down); Density, Start Distance and Height Falloff in the Inspector, saved with the scene. It hides the terrain's far edge against the sky from any camera height. A debug view shows the haze amount alone.
  • The sky: since 2026-11-09 rocky_ridge_puresky_2k.hdr (Poly Haven, CC0: no ground in the image), with the haze above and, if still needed, a low ring of distant hills from the track builder's data.

Phase 8: trees and foliage engine tools

  • Assets: three or four CC0 tree species and one or two bushes, simplified by a Blender script (Tools/TrackBuilder/prepare_tree.py): trunks and branches of a few thousand triangles, leaf cards with alpha (the Cutout surface). The script also bakes each tree's billboard atlas, its impostor (albedo with alpha and normals, seen from 8–16 directions around it).
  • Engine, a Foliage component: a list of species, density, seed, slope and height limits, a keep-out distance from the track (its center line) and from the supports; it scatters instances over the terrain.
  • Engine, instanced drawing of many copies of one mesh (scene pass, G-buffer prepass, shadow cascades), culled per instance. Distance levels: the mesh near, a billboard farther: the impostor, a card that turns to face the camera around the vertical axis only (trees stay upright seen from a crest or a jump) and shows the baked view nearest to the camera's direction, lit with its baked normals, nothing beyond, with a dithered cross-fade between them that TAA smooths. A debug view colors the instances by level.
