Driving School

The plan for a basic driving school simulator: a car with a manual gearbox (1–5, R, N), three pedals (clutch, brake, gas), a speedometer and a tachometer, a handbrake and an engine switch, driven from the driver's seat or from behind. It is made for a learner taking driving lessons, so the rules a driving school teaches come before racing effects. A vehicle physics playground (casual racing from behind the car, front-, rear- and all-wheel drive, oversteer, understeer) follows later, in the same project.

Agreed November 7, 2026, after the vehicle work (the vehicle scene, parts A and B, and the start of part C: the engine guide). Names that exist in the code are written as they are. Names of new classes, files and settings are proposals.

The decisions

  • A trainer for procedures, nothing too ambitious. It teaches the order of things and why: the start sequence (clutch, neutral, ignition, first gear), pulling away, when to shift by speed and rpm, stalling and recovering, the hill start with the handbrake, mirror and shoulder checks. It gives clear feedback when a step goes wrong ("Stalled: clutch released in 5th from a standstill"). It is not a realistic driving simulation: the feel of the pedals is learned in a real car.
  • The clutch needs no physics. A gear can only be changed while the clutch is pressed. A pressed clutch disconnects the engine from the wheels; a released clutch connects it in the chosen gear. Slip isn't modeled.
  • Controls: keyboard and mouse first, the gamepad second. There is no steering wheel set (none at hand, none planned).
  • The car is made of primitives first, with a cabin: body, pillars and roof with open windows, seats, a dashboard, a steering wheel and gauge needles. A real model can replace it later without changing the scripts.
  • The ground is the terrain of part A for now. A practice course and roads come later.
  • One project, Projects/DrivingSchool, two scenes: Driving School now, Playground later. The car, menus, settings and input are shared (projects can't share scripts). Sample stays the coin collector, with its vehicle demo scene.
  • Why the graphics looked low: not the scene. Sample's Library/Settings.yaml has Quality: Low, saved while testing Coin Collector's Settings screen, and the editor's Play uses that file. The new project defaults to High (Phase 5 measures it).

Phase 1: manual gearbox and engine switch engine

Files: src/EnvMapVulkan/EnvMapVulkanComponents.h (VehicleComponent), src/Physics/ScenePhysics.h/.cpp, src/EnvMapVulkan/EnvMapVulkanSceneSerializer.cpp, src/Editor/EditorInspectorPanel.cpp (VehicleControls), src/Scripting/ScriptApi.h, src/Scripting/ScriptBindings.cpp, ScriptCore/NativeApi.cs, ScriptCore/Components.cs.

  • VehicleComponent gains these settings, saved in the scene and shown in the Inspector:
    • Transmission: Automatic or Manual
    • GearRatios: 5 by default, 2.66, 1.78, 1.3, 1.0, 0.74 (Jolt's)
    • ReverseRatio: −2.9
    • IdleRpm: Jolt's mMinRPM
    • ClutchBitePoint: 0.5, the clutch is engaged below it
    • Stalls: the stalling rule below
  • Manual in ScenePhysics: Jolt's ETransmissionMode::Manual. Each step sets transmission.Set(gear, engaged ? 1 : 0), where gear 0 is neutral and −1 is reverse. VehicleInput gains Clutch (0..1).
  • The clutch rule: RequestGear(entity, gear) returns false and changes nothing when the clutch isn't pressed (the pedal at or past the bite point) or the gear doesn't exist. The engine enforces it, so a script, the Player controls and any later controller all behave the same.
  • The engine switch: SetEngineRunning(entity, on). Starting is refused in gear with the clutch released, as a learner is taught. Off, the engine is disconnected, the throttle is ignored and the rpm is 0.
  • Stalling, a rule, not physics: it applies with the clutch engaged in gear, when the rpm the wheels imply (wheel speed × gear ratio × differential ratio) stays below about half of idle for 0.3 s. Then the engine stops, for example on releasing the clutch in 5th from a standstill. Without the rule, Jolt holds the engine at idle and the car would be pushed along. Stalls is on by default.
  • Automatic keeps reversing by braking (part B). In Manual, Brake is only a brake.
  • VehicleState gains SelectedGear, ClutchEngaged, EngineRunning, Stalled and ShiftRefused (counters, for the HUD's messages), and MaxRpm and IdleRpm (for the gauges' scales).
  • C# Vehicle:
    • SetInput(forward, steer, brake, handbrake, clutch), with forward 0..1 in Manual
    • bool ShiftTo(int gear)
    • SelectedGear, EngineRunning (its set can be refused), Transmission, ClutchEngaged, MaxRpm
    • the stall and refused-shift counters
    New API entries are appended at the end of ScriptNativeApi and NativeApi, in the same order.
  • Debug view: the Inspector's live readout shows the gear, the clutch, the engine and the stalls.

Phase 2: the project and the car game

  • Projects/DrivingSchool:
    • DrivingSchool.mproject: windowed while it is developed, DefaultQuality: High, its own ProductName
    • Settings/Input.minput
    • Scripts/DrivingSchool.csproj, as Projects/Sample/Scripts/Sample.csproj
    • the terrain's materials from Projects/Sample/Assets/Materials/Terrain_*.mmat
  • Scenes/DrivingSchool.mscene: the terrain, the sun, the environment and the car.
  • The car is an Empty with a Collider, a Dynamic body, a Manual Vehicle and the controller script. Its children:
    • the lower body
    • four pillars and the roof, with the windows open so the driver's view sees out
    • seats and the dashboard
    • the steering wheel, a flat cylinder that turns with the steering
    • two dials with needles: 3D entities the script turns, which works today
    • four wheels, placed with Fit Wheels
  • CarController.cs (Controls: Script). The input of a driving school belongs to the game, not the engine:
    • Pedals: a held key moves its pedal gradually. Gas comes up in about 0.6 s, the clutch is released in about 0.8 s, and all pedals let go quickly. A gamepad's analog value is used as it is.
    • Keyboard steering turns the wheel gradually and brings it back to center when A and D are let go, faster the faster the car goes, as a real wheel centers itself. Keys can't steer finely, so this matters most.
    • Mouse steering is an option in Settings, off by default. The mouse's X moves the wheel; a sensitivity setting and a center key (the middle mouse button or Home) go with it. Looking around then takes the right mouse button held.
    • The mouse, by default, looks around in the driver's view: at the mirrors, over the shoulder when reversing, at crossings. Its wheel zooms the view from behind.
    • Shifting: 1–5, R, and N or 0, or gear up and down. A refused shift shows "Press the clutch to change gear".
    • The ignition, the handbrake and the steering.

Phase 3: cameras game

CarCamera.cs, with two views; C (the gamepad's Y) switches between them with a short glide.

  • Driver: the camera at the driver's head, following the car every frame. Look turns the head within limits and brings it back when let go. Near clip 0.05 for the cabin.
  • Behind: the chase camera of Projects/Sample/Scripts/VehicleCamera.cs. It turns after the car's heading, can look around and zoom, and is pulled closer by hills and walls.

Phase 4: speedometer, tachometer, HUD engine game

  • Engine: UIRectComponent.Rotation (degrees around its pivot), for needles and ticks, and useful in general.
    • The UI renderer and its hit testing (a button's hits in the turned rectangle), the serializer, the Inspector and the C# UIRect all take it.
    • It is documented in the engine guide's UI section.
  • DrivingHud.cs draws:
    • a round speedometer (km/h) and a tachometer, with ticks made of turned thin rectangles (no textures)
    • the gear (R N 1–5)
    • engine, handbrake and stall lights
    • bars for the clutch, brake and gas, so a learner sees the keyboard's pedals
    • messages
  • In the driver's view the dashboard's 3D needles move too. A setting can hide the HUD's gauges there.

Phase 5: menus, settings, quality game

  • Main menu: Driving School, Playground (disabled until its plan), Settings and Quit, adapted from Sample's MainMenu.cs.
  • Settings and pause menus: SettingsMenu.cs (with its drop-down lists), PauseMenu.cs and GameOptions.cs, copied from Projects/Sample/Scripts and adapted.
  • Quality: High by default. Measure Release at High and Ultra (the pass table of --debug-overlay) before deciding. The known remaining cost is the terrain with no level of detail: about 4.5 ms each for the scene pass and the G-buffer prepass in Debug.

Later

Each gets a plan of its own when its turn comes:

  • the Playground scene: drivetrain presets, limited-slip differentials, tire grip, weight distribution, a debug view of each wheel's slip and load
  • the rear-view and side mirrors (render textures)
  • a practice course: cones, parking spaces, a slope for hill starts
  • roads and signs
  • a real car model
  • the terrain's level of detail

How each phase is checked

  • Builds:
    • Debug and Release, editor and player
    • the GCC check of the changed C++ files (Tools/Testing/gcc-check.sh)
    • dotnet build of ScriptCore and the project's scripts
  • Unattended runs (Tools/Testing/Run-Engine.ps1) show no validation messages and no problems. A scripted driver in D:\Workspace\Temp\DrivingSchoolTest checks from the log:
    • a shift with the clutch released is refused
    • starting in gear is refused
    • neutral, then 1 to 5 with the clutch, then R
    • a release in 5th from a standstill stalls
    • with the engine off, the rpm is 0 and nothing drives
    • the keyboard's pedal and steering rates, and the wheel returning to center
  • Captures of both views and the HUD; the driver's view sees the ground through the windows.
  • The player: a pack (--build-pack) run in MoravaPlayer.
  • FPS: at High in Release, with the overlay.
  • The user tries each phase in the editor's Play.
